# Price Echo Finders per trip, not per app

Echo Finders should keep its free taste and its web-only Stripe checkout, but replace the single $6.99 "unlock everything" with two offers: a **one-time City Pass at about $9.99** for the tourist on a two-day visit, and an **All-Access annual plan at about $29.99** for frequent travellers and road-trippers. B2B commissions from tourism boards and airline licensing should be treated as the faster route to recovering the library cost. The $6.99 lifetime unlock prices below every comparable product: single self-guided audio tours sell for **$6–30, clustering at $8–15**, and Autio, the closest analogue (short GPS-triggered stories, five free, then pay), charges **about $36 a year**. A lifetime unlock also earns nothing when the second, fifth or tenth city launches. Under the current plan the library breaks even at **about 7,300–15,600 buyers** at $5.85 net. Under the recommended mix, with deliberately conservative fee assumptions, it breaks even at **about 3,200–6,800 buyers**, and the higher prices stay ahead even if conversion falls by more than half. Selling in the browser keeps about 94% of each dollar against 70–85% through Apple, and the Apple rules that would apply to a future native wrapper are still being litigated in October 2026. **A caution applies to every number below.** The research environment could not open most primary pages (App Store listings, stripe.com, RevenueCat, VoiceMap docs, the SEC filings), so most figures come from search-result snippets and must be checked before anyone relies on them. A verification table at the end lists which figures need checking.

## Ambient-story apps charge about $36 a year; tour sellers charge $10–30 per tour

The market splits by product shape. Walking and driving tour marketplaces sell discrete tours one at a time. Ambient "stories as you pass" apps, which have no fixed route and so nothing natural to sell individually, sell time-based access. Echo Finders belongs to the second group, but its multi-city tourist audience behaves like the first.

| HearHere at 2020 launch | 5 free, annual | $49.99/yr, $24.99 promo ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/fr/news-release/2020/08/05/2073499/0/en/INTRODUCING-HEARHERE-A-NEW-AUDIO-ENTERTAINMENT-APP-FOR-ROAD-TRIPS.html)) |
| VoiceMap | Per tour, bundles, credit passes; **no listener subscription** | Publishers set $5.99–29.99 per tour ([VoiceMap docs](https://docs.voicemap.me/tour-publishers/tour-price/); [VoiceMap support](https://support.voicemap.me/is-voicemap-a-subscription-service/)) |

Three patterns matter for Echo Finders. First, **Autio's 30-day price ($29.99) sits almost on top of its annual price ($35.99)**, and it also sells a two-week plan. That structure targets the single road trip and uses the annual plan as the anchor. Second, Autio also sells three-year, five-year and "lifetime" deals through StackSocial-style resellers ([StackSocial](https://www.stacksocial.com/sales/autio-unlimited-plan-3-year-subscription)). That suggests renewals among occasional travellers are weak and that cash up front is worth discounting for. Third, free anchors are real. Rick Steves gives European city walks away, and museum and DMO-sponsored tours are often free. So the free echoes must show that Echo Finders is better than free, not merely different.

The cautionary precedent is Detour. It sold premium, carefully produced walks at about **$5 a tour or $20 a year**, then was bought by Bose and shut down in 2018 after making all tours free ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/04/24/bose-acquires-andrew-masons-walking-tour-startup-detour); [AFAR](https://www.afar.com/magazine/our-favorite-walking-tour-app-is-totally-free-through-may-31)). Low consumer price points did not carry the cost of premium content. Every player in the category is small: Autio raised **$5.9M led by iHeartMedia** in 2023 ([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/Autio-raises-millions-expand-mobile-storytelling-reach)), and Shaka Guide reported about 400,000 downloads in 2022 ([Hawaii News Now](https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com/2022/09/21/people-behind-wildly-popular-shaka-guide-are-taking-national-parks)). Nobody dominates, and nobody has shown that consumer sales alone build a large business here.

## Expect 2–5% of free users to pay, and expect them to buy around trips

The base rate for a metered freemium model is low. RevenueCat's 2026 data, reported second-hand, puts median download-to-paid conversion by day 35 at **2.1% for freemium apps against 10.7% for hard paywalls**. Revenue per install at day 60 is **$0.38 against $3.09** ([Subscription Insider](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/article-type/news/revenuecat-data-shows-subscription-app-growth-concentrating-at-the-top)). Paid podcast products convert **2–7% of engaged listeners** ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/podcasts/paid-podcasts-subscriber-conversion-rate/)). Museums charging extra for audio guides see about **3% take-up**, but **40–60%** when the guide is included in admission, as at English Heritage ([Claudio Buda](https://claudiobuda.substack.com/p/the-audio-guide-nobody-buys-but-everybody); [Nubart](https://www.nubart.eu/audio-guides/museum-audio-guide-app-adoption-rates.html)). "Ten free echoes, then pay" is a post-content, metered paywall, so the honest planning range is **2% base case, 4–5% good, above 8% exceptional**.

Travel users who reach a purchase decision mostly have intent. Travel has among the highest median trial-to-paid rates of any category, at about **43.5% (2026) to 48.7% (earlier report)** ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps); [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)). The bottleneck is getting a visitor to the offer before the trip ends. A paywall at echo 10 fails silently for a tourist who hears seven echoes and flies home. Paywall-timing data from vendor blogs, which could not be traced to a primary dataset, points the same way: upfront paywalls convert about **12% against just over 2%** for post-content ones ([dev.to / PaywallPro](https://dev.to/paywallpro/the-paywall-timing-paradox-why-showing-your-price-upfront-can-5x-your-conversions-4alc)). The best trigger comes right after a "value moment" ([Setgreet](https://www.setgreet.com/blog/onboarding-paywall-placement)).

Subscription versus one-time is a question about segments. Travel subscribers overwhelmingly choose **annual plans (about 65–66%)**, and Travel has the **best median annual renewal rate of any category, at 40%** ([RevenueCat renewal benchmarks](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/average-subscription-renewal-rates-by-app-category)). That still means about 60% leave at first renewal. RevenueCat also reports **60–77% churn at first renewal** for annual plans generally ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/first-renewal-churn)), and notes that lifetime and one-time purchases are common in Travel ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/lifetime-subscriptions)). Adapty saw one-time purchases grow 6% in 2025 as users "leaned into flexibility" ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/state-of-in-app-subscriptions-2025-in-10-minutes/)). The Met's audio-guide users are **81% tourists and 19% locals**, the locals being likely repeat visitors or members ([The Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/perspectives/improving-the-audio-guide-a-look-at-our-visitors)). Read together, the evidence says one-off tourists should get a one-time city or trip purchase with no renewal to resent. Frequent travellers and road-trippers fit an annual plan. Locals are a small revenue segment, better used for word of mouth than as a target for monetisation. No 2024–2026 willingness-to-pay survey (Van Westendorp or similar) for self-guided city audio was found. Price levels here are therefore inferred from market prices, not measured.

## Selling in the browser keeps about 94 cents of each dollar while Apple's rules stay in court

On Stripe directly, a US card sale costs **2.9% + $0.30**. International cards add 1.5% and currency conversion 1%, Stripe Billing adds 0.7% for subscriptions, and Stripe Tax adds 0.5% ([Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/stripe-fees-calculator); [checkoutpage.com](https://checkoutpage.com/blog/stripe-processing-fees)). These figures come from secondary sources because stripe.com/pricing was blocked. Merchants of record, which take on worldwide VAT and sales-tax liability, cost more: **Paddle at about 5% + $0.50** ([Vendr](https://www.vendr.com/buyer-guides/paddle)) and **Stripe Managed Payments at about 6.4% + $0.30** ([Paritydeals](https://www.paritydeals.com/stripe-managed-payments-fees/)). Lemon Squeezy's terms conflict between sources. The fixed $0.30 fee hurts small tickets: it is 4.3% of $6.99 but 3.0% of $9.99. On Stripe direct, $6.99 nets about **$6.49**, so the plan's **$5.85** already allows for international cards, tax and refunds, or for a merchant of record. That conservative figure is sensible, because EU VAT on digital services is owed from the first euro of sales to consumers.

| Channel, $9.99 sale | Kept (approx.) | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Stripe direct, US card | **$9.40 (94%)** | 2.9% + $0.30 |
| Paddle (merchant of record) | $8.99 (90%) | 5% + $0.50 |
| Apple IAP, Small Business Program | $8.49 (85%) | 15% ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)) |
| Apple IAP, standard | $6.99 (70%) | 30% |
| EU wrapper link-out to Stripe, small developer | about $8.40–8.50 | 10% Apple plus Stripe fees ([phiture](https://phiture.com/blog/apple-new-app-store-terms-2026/)) |

As a browser PWA, Echo Finders pays Apple and Google nothing, and that remains the core advantage of the web-first decision. The rules matter only if a native wrapper is added later for background audio and location. **In the US**, apps may link to web checkout with no Apple commission since 30 April 2025, but that position is unsettled. The Ninth Circuit upheld the contempt finding in December 2025 but said Apple may eventually charge a court-approved, non-prohibitive fee ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/25-2935/25-2935-2025-12-11.html)). In August 2026 Apple proposed link-out fees of **15% standard, 10% for partner programmes and renewals, and 5% for small businesses**, which Epic rejected ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/14/apple-proposes-to-take-a-15-cut-of-purchases-made-outside-the-app-store/); [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/13/app-store-fees-apple-link-outs/)). The Supreme Court granted certiorari on 30 June 2026, limited to the civil-contempt standard, and Apple filed its merits brief on 14 September 2026 ([SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/cases/apple-inc-v-epic-games-inc-2/)). Plan for a US link-out fee of 5–15% at some point, and re-check before any wrapper ships. **In the EU**, Apple's unified terms took effect on **1 October 2026**: **26% with IAP, 20% with an alternative payment provider, 15% on link-outs** (10% for small developers or eligible programmes), and a **5% Core Technology Commission** for apps distributed outside the App Store ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/18/apple-overhauls-app-store-fees-in-the-eu-with-new-unified-terms/); [MacObserver](https://www.macobserver.com/news/eu-app-store-terms-october-1-rate-card/)). **Google Play** is moving in the US, UK and EEA to 10% plus 5% for Play Billing on subscriptions, and 20–25% on in-app purchases ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/google-play-billing-changes-subscriptions-fees/)). A wrapper could carry web purchases over as a "reader app", since guideline 3.1.3(a) explicitly covers audio, or under the multiplatform rule 3.1.3(b), which requires the same items to be sold through IAP as well ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Reader-app status for a location-triggered story app has no precedent, so treat it as a hypothesis for App Review, not a plan.

Web checkout has a conversion cost. RevenueCat's one controlled test (the Dipsea app, May 2025) found IAP converting at **about 28% against about 18%** for a link-out web flow, with most of the drop between payment sheet and purchase ([Daring Fireball](https://daringfireball.net/linked/2025/05/15/revenuecat-external-purchase-report)). For a PWA the comparison is web checkout against no purchase at all, so the lesson is to make checkout one tap: **Apple Pay and Google Pay first**, which Stripe supports in Safari at no extra fee ([Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/apple-pay.md)).

## B2B buyers pay for production and placement, not per play

Airlines buy IFE content through flat fees per window, through usage fees ("based on number of flights or number of aircraft or pay-per-view", in Global Eagle's 10-K) ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1512077/000151207720000006/ent-10kdocument4q19.htm)), and through newer **per-aircraft-per-month subscriptions** such as Anuvu's Iris. Iris pricing scales with fleet size, how often content is refreshed and how many channels the airline takes ([Runway Girl Network](https://runwaygirlnetwork.com/2021/04/global-eagle-seeks-to-reinvent-ife-with-subscription-service-for-airlines/)). Hollywood benchmarks such as about **$90,000 per film for a roughly three-month window** at large carriers ([CNN Travel](https://www.cnn.com/travel/article/secrets-of-in-flight-movies/index.html)) show the budget exists, but they are not comparable to niche audio. No per-aircraft price for any geo-story or point-of-interest layer was found. The closest precedent, **Autio on JetBlue seatbacks since February 2023** (3–5 minute stories, about 29 cities, 250+ stories per city), has undisclosed terms ([Simple Flying](https://simpleflying.com/jetblue-announces-partnership-with-autio/)). The moving-map vendors already own the screen Echo Finders wants to reach: Panasonic Arc runs on **40+ airlines and 1,300+ aircraft** with editable points-of-interest layers ([Runway Girl Network](https://runwaygirlnetwork.com/2026/06/panasonic-avionics-arc-3d-in-flight-map-passes-40-airline-customers/)), and FlightPath3D on 50+ airlines ([Runway Girl Network](https://runwaygirlnetwork.com/2018/12/05/with-ana-as-50th-customer-flightpath3d-continues-growth-trajectory/)). The practical route is to supply stories to these vendors or to content service providers (Spafax, Anuvu, Stellar) under a flat annual licence or a per-aircraft-per-month fee, not to compete with the map. Airline revenue should be modelled as zero until a contract is signed; the sales cycle and intermediary margins are not published anywhere found.

Tourism boards are the best-documented B2B buyer, and their prices look nothing like consumer prices. VoiceMap quotes **$4,000–10,000 for one commissioned, fully produced tour**, and multi-tour packages have exceeded **$50,000** ([VoiceMap pricing](https://voicemap.me/pricing); [VoiceMap case study](https://blog.voicemap.me/2025/11/audio-tour-case-study-creating-exceptional-self-guided-audio-tours-at-scale/)). TravelStorys charges about **$10,000 for set-up and the first year**, then a lower annual fee, with tours free to listeners ([ETV News](https://etvnews.com/articles/local-news/price-and-helper-join-together-to-bring-travelstorys-to-the-area/)). Public heritage-trail contracts that include an app reach six figures: Williamsburg, Virginia awarded **$297,865** for an app, map and community engagement ([CitizenPortal](https://citizenportal.ai/articles/5931319/virginia/james-city-county/williamsburg-city/williamsburg-awards-297865-contract-to-develop-african-american-heritage-trail-app-map-and-community-engagement)). Autio has announced partnerships with the Texas State Historical Association and Victoria's tourism council, but not their value ([CultureMap](https://dallas.culturemap.com/news/travel/autio-app-texas-history-costner)). Echo Finders' library costs about **$8.40–17.60 per echo** ($42k–88k over 5,000). A VoiceMap-style tour of 10–20 stops priced at $4k–10k works out to roughly $200–1,000 per stop. The two are not like for like, since a commissioned tour is a scripted route and an echo is 90 seconds, but the gap is large enough that one DMO commission of about $7,500 equals roughly **1,280 consumer unlocks at $5.85**. For hotels, car rental and rail, no disclosed deals or prices were found, only white-label and SDK offers from audio-guide vendors ([Futurefestival catalogue](https://www.futurefestival.com/trends/catalogue-of-audio-guides)). Treat those as exploratory.

## Recommended structure: $9.99 City Pass plus $29.99 All-Access, breaking even near 3,200–6,800 buyers

The recommended launch structure has four parts. First, keep a **free taste**, but count it per city and test fewer than 10 echoes (see below). Second, sell a **City Pass at $9.99**, a one-time purchase that unlocks one city permanently, including echoes added there later. It sits inside the $8–15 band for a single tour, below a free-walking-tour tip of €10–20 ([Riga Free Tours](https://www.rigafreetours.com/post/how-much-to-tip-on-a-free-walking-tour)), and it does not auto-renew, so tourists have nothing to cancel. Third, sell **All-Access at $29.99 a year**, every city plus road-trip and in-flight modes, auto-renewing annually. It is priced under Autio's $35.99 and is shown beside the City Pass so that three cities makes it the obvious choice. That anchoring pattern is the one SmartGuide uses, where the annual plan costs about 2.7 months of the monthly price. Fourth, keep checkout on **Stripe in the browser with Apple Pay and Google Pay first**. Move to a merchant of record such as Paddle once EU and UK sales are material, unless Echo Finders is prepared to register for VAT itself. The single $6.99 lifetime unlock is the option to retire. It undercuts the market, it collects nothing from the growth the multi-city plan depends on, and the fixed card fee takes a bigger share of a small ticket.

**Break-even under the current plan.** The cost to recover in year one is the one-time library plus 12 months of running costs: **$42,000 + $900 = $42,900** at the low end and **$88,000 + $3,000 = $91,000** at the high end. At $5.85 net per buyer:

- Low end: $42,900 ÷ $5.85 = **7,334 buyers**.
- High end: $91,000 ÷ $5.85 = **15,556 buyers**.

At an assumed **3% conversion** of activated users (users who play at least one echo), that needs **about 244,000–519,000 activated users**. At 2%, the freemium median, it needs **about 367,000–778,000**. Running costs alone need 154–513 new buyers a year at $5.85.

**Break-even under the recommended structure.** To stay comparable with the $5.85 plan, which keeps 84% of $6.99, the same **84% net factor** is applied to the new prices. That is conservative: Stripe direct would keep about 94% of $9.99 and 96% of $29.99. The resulting nets are $9.99 × 0.84 = **$8.39** and $29.99 × 0.84 = **$25.19**. Assume **70% of buyers take the City Pass and 30% take All-Access**:

- Blended net per buyer: (0.70 × $8.39) + (0.30 × $25.19) = $5.87 + $7.56 = **$13.43**.
- Low end: $42,900 ÷ $13.43 = **3,195 buyers**.
- High end: $91,000 ÷ $13.43 = **6,776 buyers**.

Higher prices will lower conversion. Assume conversion falls by a fifth, from 3% to **2.4%**. Break-even then needs **about 133,000–282,000 activated users**, roughly half the audience the current plan needs. Revenue per activated user rises from 0.03 × $5.85 = **$0.176** to 0.024 × $13.43 = **$0.322**. The new structure only does worse if conversion falls below $0.176 ÷ $13.43 = **1.31%**, a drop of more than 56% from the 3% base. Year-two renewals add revenue the current plan does not have. At Travel's median 40% annual renewal, every 1,000 All-Access buyers bring back about 400 renewals, or roughly $10,000 net. Running costs alone need only about 67–224 buyers a year at the blended net.

These figures rest on assumptions that need testing. The 70/30 mix is a guess. The 3% and 2.4% rates are drawn from the freemium, podcast and museum benchmarks above, not from audio-tour data, which no company publishes. The 84% net factor is borrowed from the existing plan. One tourism-board commission at $5k–10k covers the cost of roughly 370–745 consumer buyers at the blended net, so **two to five DMO deals in the launch cities could recover a large share of the library cost** before consumer sales do. That makes the human fact-check spend look less like a sunk cost and more like inventory that can be sold more than once.

**What to test first, in order.** The first test is the **free allowance and the moment of the offer**, because conversion hinges on whether tourists ever reach the paywall. Measure how many activated users reach echo 10 within their visit. Then run an A/B test of 10 against 5 free echoes, with the offer shown at the first echo and again straight after a strong echo ends, not only at the limit. The second test is **price**: $6.99 against $9.99 for the City Pass, with All-Access shown next to it, judged on revenue per activated user rather than conversion rate. The third test is **what buyers choose**: City Pass only, All-Access only, or both together. That shows the real mix and whether a 30-day trip pass at about $14.99 is worth adding, as Autio does. In parallel, the first B2B step is to **pitch one tourism board in a launch city** on a commissioned pack priced at $5k–10k, with a lower annual hosting fee after year one. That tests the strongest revenue evidence in this research faster than any airline conversation will.

## Conclusion

The pricing question turns out to be mostly a timing question. Every benchmark says travellers with intent convert well once they face an offer. The risk for Echo Finders is a tourist who leaves town before hearing echo 10, not a $9.99 price that is too high. So the free allowance, the first-echo offer and one-tap Apple Pay matter more than the dollar figure, and they should be tested before anyone argues over $6.99 against $9.99. The second insight is that the library is the asset and consumers are only one buyer. Tourism boards already pay hundreds of dollars per produced stop for content that Echo Finders makes for about $8–18 per echo. B2B commissions are therefore likely to fund the library sooner than consumer sales, and they make Echo Finders' fact-check spend sellable rather than sunk.

The regulatory picture favours staying in the browser. As long as the web PWA is the product, Apple and Google rules cost nothing. Any native wrapper should wait until the US link-out fee is settled and should be built around genuinely native features, background audio and location, so that it can pass App Review's guideline 4.2, which rejects apps that are little more than a repackaged website.
