# Win ice cream through the case, not the cone

Build it, but not as a straight Untappd clone. The opening is real. At least eight small apps already let people log and rate ice cream, and none has visible scale, funding or press. The thing that makes Untappd work, a stable catalogue of products checked in at venues, does not exist for scoop shops, because their flavours rotate daily and monthly. The way in is to own the question every scoop-shop customer already asks: "what's in the case today, and is it any good?" That means flavour-level logging tied to a dated sighting at a shop, fed by photos of the flavour board read by a vision model. It should launch in one dense metro in May, through a state or county ice cream trail. The economics are tighter than beer. There are about **12,800 US ice cream stores** against Untappd's roughly **15,000 paying venues at about $900–$1,200 a year**, and scoop shops run on sub-$100-a-month software budgets. Shop subscriptions alone therefore cannot fund a venture-scale company. The more promising early money is sponsored badges from pint brands that launch limited flavours constantly, plus trail and passport contracts with tourism and dairy boards. Treat this as a capital-light product that earns the right to grow, perhaps into all desserts as Untappd has into all beverages, rather than as a venture bet sized on the $7B scoop-shop market. **A note on evidence:** every research pass in this project was blocked from fetching most primary pages. Nearly all Untappd and competitor figures, and the IDFA, Circana/Dairy Foods and USDA NASS market figures, come from search-result summaries of the cited pages, not from reading those pages in full. Figures marked **(search summary)** most need checking before they go into a pitch.

## Untappd turned a Foursquare habit into a $900-a-venue SaaS business

Untappd's atomic unit is the check-in: one drink, optionally tied to a venue, a rating, a photo and notes. The founders, Greg Avola and Tim Mather, were heavy Foursquare users and copied its check-in behaviour "with more emphasis on what check-ins could do for beer drinkers" ([Braze](https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/engage-millions-users-mobile-community-building-lessons-untappd), search summary; [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2016/04/25/two-guys-created-their-dream-jobs-forged-by-a-love-for-tech-and-beer.html)). The app launched publicly in October 2010 after a September beta (Wikipedia, search summary). It hit **1 million check-ins and bought rival RedPint in 2011** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=311066)), reached **500 million check-ins in 2017** ([Untappd blog](https://blog.untappd.com/post/165869743736/cheers-to-500000000-check-ins)) and **1 billion in April 2021** ([Untappd blog](https://untappd.com/blog/we-re-about-to-hit-one-billion-check-ins/1214)). By November 2025 it had passed **1.5 billion check-ins at more than 1 million venues** ([The Drinks Business](https://www.thedrinksbusiness.com/2025/11/untappd-expands-beyond-the-beer-category/), search summary). Its December 2025 recap reached **nearly 12 million users and logged nearly 100 million check-ins that year** ([Recappd release reprint](https://rcnamericancsc.blogspot.com/2025/12/untappd-launches-untappd-recappd.html)). That works out to about eight check-ins per user per year, which means a small core of heavy "tickers" carries a long casual tail. About 100 million check-ins a year at maturity also reads as a plateau rather than a growth curve. In November 2025 Untappd opened to all packaged beverages, which suggests beer alone had hit a ceiling.

The engine underneath is a **crowdsourced catalogue with governance**. Users add missing beers. Anyone can "Propose Duplicate", and the proposal goes to a regional volunteer moderator. Breweries claim their pages for free, then curate and merge their own listings ([Untappd Help](https://help.untappd.com/hc/en-us/articles/360033797752-Breweries-How-do-I-manage-my-beers-on-Untappd)). The catalogue has a cost: merges silently cut users' "unique beer" counts ([Untappd Help](https://help.untappd.com/hc/en-us/articles/360034134252-My-unique-beer-count-has-decreased-all-of-a-sudden-What-happened)), and community reports say even senior moderators cannot reverse a merge ([r/Untappd mirror](https://lr.psf.lt/r/Untappd/comments/1jyv79h/weekly_edit_request_post_20250414)). Badges with levels and yearly seasonal sets drive repeat logging ([Untappd Lounge](https://lounge.untappd.com/everything-you-need-to-know-about-untappd-badges)). The annual "Recappd" works as both a growth event and a sponsorship slot.

Untappd makes money three ways. The first and most important is **Untappd for Business**, venue software for live menus, TV boards, websites and POS sync. Third-party sources put it at **about $899/year (Essentials) or $1,199/year (Premium)** ([BeerMenus](https://www.beermenus.com/blog/260-guide-to-untappd-for-business), a competitor; [Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/untappd-for-business/pricing)). The official page gives no prices and asks for a quote ([UTFB](https://utfb.untappd.com/get-pricing/)). Only paying venues get "Verified" status and a live menu in the app. One source, a Toast help page, puts the customer base at **about 15,000 bars and breweries** ([Toast support](https://support.toasttab.com/zh-Hans/article/Get-Started-With-the-Untappd-for-Business-Integration)). That implies roughly $13–18M a year from venue software, which is my own estimate. The second is **brand marketing**: sponsored badges, in-app promotions and home-screen placements reaching more than **43M monthly impressions** ([Untappd marketing](https://marketing.untappd.com/b2c-marketing-solutions/)). Prices were reported at **$2,000/month for a national sponsored badge, $10,000 for in-app promotions and $30,000 for home-screen placement**, though the date is uncertain and the figures may be stale ([Brewbound](https://www.brewbound.com/news/untappds-parent-company-acquires-beer-advocate)). Untappd says sponsors see a 50–100% lift in check-ins, which is its own claim. The third is **Insiders**, a consumer subscription at $5.99/month or $54.99/year ([Untappd Help](https://help.untappd.com/hc/en-us/articles/4409476745492-What-are-my-pricing-options-to-become-an-Untappd-Insider)).

Ownership shows where the value ended up. Next Glass acquired Untappd in January 2016 ([Porch Drinking](https://porchdrinking.com/articles/2016/01/15/next-glass-acquires-untapped-in-symbiotic-merger/)), took private-equity growth money from Providence Strategic Growth in 2020, and bought BeerAdvocate, Hop Culture and ordering marketplaces ([WilmingtonBiz](https://www.wilmingtonbiz.com/investors_pour_money_into_next_glass/2020/04/23/investors_pour_money_into_next_glass/20284); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/chrisfurnari/2020/02/26/beer-advocate-assets-sold-to-untappd-parent-company-next-glass/)). Revenue is undisclosed, and third-party estimates run from $8M to $100M ([Datanyze](https://www.datanyze.com/companies/next-glass/371619939); [LeadIQ](https://leadiq.com/c/next-glass/5a1d82b324000024005d5403)). The lesson is that the consumer app is the acquisition funnel and B2B software is the business. Untappd's known weaknesses carry over too. Ratings inflate for fashionable styles ([r/NashvilleBeer](https://reddit.fsky.io/r/NashvilleBeer/comments/1j9lifc/meta_uptappd_ratings)). Ticker culture rewards novelty over enjoyment ([VinePair](https://vinepair.com/articles/untapped-impact-craft-beer/)). And the cost of verification draws complaints from small venues ([The Brewer Magazine](https://thebrewermagazine.com/tapped-or-untappd-is-verified-venue-cost-worth-it/)).

## Eight indie ice cream apps exist and none has broken out

The ice cream check-in space is crowded but empty of winners. Apps split into two shapes. The first is **flavour-at-a-shop logging**. Scooped, from a Dutch developer, rates a flavour separately from the parlour and claims more than 5,000 parlours across 16 countries ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.scooped.mobile), search summary). The Scoop, a US app, rates flavours and shops on a live map and wins users with "a free scoop for your first review" ([scoopapp.io](https://scoopapp.io/)). Treatly is a "Letterboxd for food"-style journal with stats and a map ([treatly.app](https://www.treatly.app/best-ice-cream-journal-app)). dipt ([dipt.app](https://www.dipt.app/)), Dippd ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dippd/id6451426152)), ScoopQuest ([AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/6761337214)) and Rate My Scoop ([rate-my-scoop.com](https://rate-my-scoop.com/)) are similar. The second shape is **packaged-pint rating**. IceCreamRatings.com calls itself the largest ice cream community and has about 10,000 mostly packaged products ([icecreamratings.com](https://icecreamratings.com/), search summary). No app does both well. Dippd had too few App Store ratings to show a score. No app showed traction data, and no post-mortems turned up. These apps seem to stay small and quiet rather than fail in public. The conclusion is that **building the app is easy and distribution is the unsolved problem**. Copying the features offers no moat.

Chains have built single-brand versions of the same need. Sweet Cow's app shows each shop's current flavour board and alerts users when a favourite returns, but reviews complain that the board did not match the case ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sweet-cow/id1210614210)). Culver's publishes a "Flavor of the Day" calendar for each store. Bruster's and Cold Stone have their own apps ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/brusters/id1132180307)). That chains pay to build this themselves shows the demand is real. The Sweet Cow complaint shows that **stale flavour data destroys trust faster than missing data**.

Adjacent "Untappd for X" apps show which conditions produce a winner:

| App | Category | Core mechanic | Monetisation | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Untappd | Beer | Check-in + badges + venue menus | Venue SaaS, brand ads, subscription | ~12M users; PE-backed roll-up |
| Vivino | Wine | Label scan | Marketplace commission, Premium | 65–71M+ users, 200K+ Premium (CEO, 2024, figures inconsistent); $155M Series D ([ExpandedRamblings](https://expandedramblings.com/index.php/vivino-facts-statistics), search summary; [Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/university/vivino-pours-155m-into-series-d/?format=md)) |
| Beli | Restaurants | Pairwise "better or worse than X" ranking | Undisclosed | 75M+ ratings (Sept 2025), 80% of users under 35 ([InsideHook](https://www.insidehook.com/food/does-dining-out-count-beli-app); [Food Network](https://www.foodnetwork.com/fn-dish/news/beli-app-trend)) |
| Delectable | Wine | Label scan, social | Sold to Vinous Media, 2016 | Faded in relevance ([Vinography](https://www.vinography.com/2016/12/wine_app_wars_continue)) |

The winners had a large structured catalogue and a fast logging loop, and they made money later through producers and venues or through commerce. Small connoisseur categories stayed at tens of thousands of users. Beli matters most for ice cream. Its pairwise ranking gives value from only a handful of entries, and about **80% of its growth came from word of mouth** ([Ivey Business Review](https://www.iveybusinessreview.ca/magazine/articles/beli)). That suits a category where a keen user might log four scoops a month. Shop-level discovery already belongs to Yelp and Google Maps. A new app should win at the level of the flavour, not the shop.

## A $7B scoop-shop market that is mature, seasonal and churning flavours monthly

The market is large but not growing. Circana data via Dairy Foods puts US grocery ice cream at **about $7.5B for the year to November 2025, down 1%, with units down 3%** ([Dairy Foods](https://dairyfoods.com/articles/96975-ice-cream-sales-stay-hot), search summary). Frozen novelties add **about $9.2B** ([Dairy Foods](https://www.dairyfoods.com/articles/98029-novelty-sales-produce-small-gains), search summary). The year-on-year Circana series are not like-for-like: a 2024 figure of $8.65B sits oddly against a reported 1% decline to $7.5B. US scoop shops took **about $7.4B in 2025 across 12,781 businesses**, a count growing about 4% a year since 2020, with **low market concentration**; Cold Stone is the largest player ([IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/number-of-businesses/ice-cream-stores/4317); [IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/market-size/ice-cream-shops/4317/)). Volume has fallen for two decades, from a 2003 peak of 1,542M gallons to 1,386M in 2024 ([USDA ERS](https://ers.usda.gov/data-products/charts-of-note/112892)). Price is carrying the dollar figures. **Superpremium pints outperform the category** ([Frozen & Refrigerated Buyer](https://frb.mydigitalpublication.com/articles/rocky-road-for-ice-cream)). The craft chains are expanding: Jeni's has more than 90 shops, Salt & Straw 40–50 and is reportedly weighing a sale above $200M, and Van Leeuwen targets about 100 ([rallies.ai](https://rallies.ai/news/jenis-splendid-ice-creams-names-ex-ben-jerrys-ceo-to-drive-90-store-growth); [restaurantassociation.com](https://restaurantassociation.com/posts/salt-straw-eyes-200m-deal-to-fund-growth/)). This is a mature market whose premium, craft end is growing, much like craft beer when Untappd launched. The app should aim at that premium end, not at mass-market tubs. Global estimates vary too widely to use, from **$71B to $121B for 2025** ([IMARC](https://imarcgroup.com/ice-cream-market); [Grand View via Dairy Industries](https://www.dairyindustries.com/?p=50757)).

Seasonality is the biggest structural difference from beer. Visits to ice cream chains hit a 4.5-year high in May 2024. The summer spike is much sharper in colder states such as New York than in Florida ([Placer.ai](https://placer.ai/blog/frozen-delights-exploring-ice-cream-chains-across-america)). Ice cream and frozen yogurt took **2.1% of US dining visits in June–July 2026** ([Placer.ai](https://www.placer.ai/anchor/articles/frozen-yogurts-comeback-is-more-than-nostalgia), search summary). Per-person consumption of regular ice cream is about **12 lb a year, down about 25% since 2000** ([Dairy Processing](https://www.dairyprocessing.com/articles/4390-state-of-the-industry-premium-scoops-innovation-redefining-ice-cream-aisle)). An app in a northern market should expect winter usage to fall sharply. Pints, eaten at home all year, are the natural way to fill that gap.

Flavour rotation is the category's best feature for an app and its hardest data problem. Salt & Straw **debuts five new limited flavours every month** in themed series: Cereal-sly in March 2026, Vault reissues, a Chef Series in June 2026 ([Salt & Straw](https://saltandstraw.com/products/pints-of-the-month-ice-scream-series); [BroadwayWorld](https://www.broadwayworld.com/article/SALT-STRAW-with-2-Locations-in-NYC-Offers-Cereal-sly-Delicious-Series-20260228)). Ben & Jerry's has retired **more than 200 flavours** and sometimes brings them back ([Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/food-drink/visit-to-the-ben-jerrys-flavor-graveyard)). That is the same "new release" churn that keeps Untappd tickers checking in, and it gives people a reason to come back. Mainstream taste is conservative. IDFA's 2026 survey of more than 2,200 adults put chocolate back at #1, with butter pecan passing vanilla ([IDFA](https://www.idfa.org/news/chocolate-returns-to-the-top-as-americas-favorite-flavor-according-to-idfas-national-ice-cream-trends-survey), search summary). **97% of consumers love or like ice cream** ([Dairy Foods](https://www.dairyfoods.com/articles/97332-idfa-97-of-consumers-love-or-like-ice-cream), search summary). The adventurous flavour-chasing segment is a minority, and none of the research quantified it.

There is evidence of enthusiast demand, but it sits on other platforms. r/icecream has about 130K members, up about 43% year on year (GummySearch estimate) ([GummySearch](https://gummysearch.com/r/icecream/)). Dylan Lemay has 11.2M followers and raised $1.5M to open a shop ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2021/11/16/tiktoker-dylan-lemay-funding-ice-cream-shop/)). One TikTok review of a Secaucus shop drew **2M views and lines down the block, and tubs sold out within about a day** ([Patch](https://patch.com/new-jersey/secaucus/secaucus-ice-cream-shop-has-lines-down-block-after-tiktok-review)). The strongest behavioural evidence is the **state ice cream trail**. Maryland's trail had handed out **28,000 paper passports** by 2015, and nearly 400 families completed it ([Maryland Dept. of Agriculture](https://news.maryland.gov/mda/press-release/2015/05/29/marylands-best-ice-cream-trail-opens-for-2015/)). Pennsylvania's "Scooped" trail ran **59 creameries in 2026, its eighth year, on a Bandwango digital passport** ([VisitPA](https://www.visitpa.com/trip/scooped-ice-cream-trail); [MyChesCo](https://www.mychesco.com/a/news/pennsylvania/this-pennsylvania-tradition-is-bigger-than-ice-cream/)). County trails run in Indiana, Ohio and Pennsylvania. These trails prove that "visit N creameries, earn a reward" motivates people, and that public bodies already pay for the software.

## Rotating flavours break Untappd's catalogue, and five design choices fix it

Ice cream differs from beer in four ways that matter. Each has an answer the research supports.

**Flavour identity.** "Cookies & Cream" exists at every shop, and Tuesday's case differs from Saturday's. Beer has stable products; ice cream has recipes that appear and disappear. The fix is a model with two levels plus time. At the top is a **Maker**, a brand or a shop. The Maker owns **Flavours**, each tagged with a style ("cookies & cream", "mint chip") and a base (dairy, vegan, gelato, sorbet). A Flavour comes in **Formats** (a scoop at a shop, a pint with a UPC). A **Sighting** records a location, a date, a source and a confidence level. Check-ins attach to a flavour, a place and a date. Generic styles should be tags that roll up across makers ("best cookies & cream in Austin"), never merged entities. That avoids the irreversible merges and shrinking counts that trouble Untappd ([Untappd Help](https://help.untappd.com/hc/en-us/articles/360034035852)). A seasonal return, such as pumpkin every October, reuses the same flavour with a new availability window, like a wine vintage.

**Cold-start data.** No database of scoop-shop flavours exists. Shop locations can come from places APIs such as Foursquare's taxonomy, though its category IDs change between taxonomy versions ([Foursquare](https://docs.foursquare.com/places/docs/categories)). Pints are only partly covered by Open Food Facts, where the US "ice cream bars" category has just 172 products, many with generic names ([Open Food Facts](https://us.openfoodfacts.org/category/en:ice-cream-bars)). POS menus probably will not help, because many shops ring up "2 scoops" rather than flavours. That is an inference and needs checking in shop interviews. The workable seed is **photos of the flavour case or chalkboard, read by a vision model behind a confidence gate**. DoorDash already runs this in production: OCR plus an LLM, with a classifier that sends low-confidence transcriptions to humans ([ByteByteGo on DoorDash](https://blog.bytebytego.com/p/how-doordash-uses-ai-models-to-understand)). Hand-lettered boards and glare behind glass are exactly the hard cases DoorDash describes, so plan for a lot of human review at first. Vivino needed **about two years** before its catalogue made the product meaningful, and its founder photographed bottles by hand ([CNN Money](https://money.cnn.com/2017/08/01/smallbusiness/vivino-wine-app/index.html), search summary). Ice cream is smaller: one metro is a few hundred shops, so a founder could seed it by hand in weeks.

**Frequency.** A cone a week is an intense user, and scoop visits cluster in summer, so daily streaks would punish normal behaviour. Gamification should be built on **collections and seasons**: city or state passports, "Summer 2027: 10 shops" badges, flavour bingo by style, and credit for being the first to log a new flavour, which also feeds the catalogue. Badges should reward exploration rather than volume. Untappd's repeat-check-in badges already draw criticism ([Porch Drinking](https://porchdrinking.com/articles/2017/03/01/why-i-stopped-rating-beers-on-untappd/)), and an "eat five pints" badge aimed at children would be a reputational risk. Beli-style pairwise ranking gives a user with only six scoops a satisfying, shareable ranked list. Rating inflation for fashionable flavours, like stouts on Untappd, can be offset by showing each score against the average for its style.

**Audience and age.** Ice cream needs no age gate and carries no alcohol advertising rules. It is family-friendly and very photogenic, all of which widen the market. But Untappd is rated 17+ ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/no/app/untappd/id449141888?l=nb)) and ice cream will attract children, so COPPA obligations apply. Design profiles and the feed for under-13s from day one: an age screen, no public child profiles, and limited data collection.

On technology, a **web PWA is viable for the MVP**. Photo capture, geolocation and rating all work in the browser, and on iOS 16.4+ push notifications work once the app is installed to the home screen. The limits are that push requires that install, push permission acceptance is **about 16%**, iOS has no background geofencing, and EU push is restricted ([MagicBell](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/pwa-ios-limitations-safari-support-complete-guide)). All of these sources are vendor blogs, not Apple documentation. The web suits shop staff, who will not install an app to post a board. A native wrapper is needed later for alerts like "Pumpkin is back at the shop 0.3 mi away".

## Pint brands and trails pay before scoop shops do

The obvious model, Untappd for Business for scoop shops, runs into arithmetic. Small shops use Square with no subscription, and larger parlours pay Toast $69 a month or more. Total POS spend runs from nothing to about $100 a month ([Kitchen Business](https://kitchenbusiness.com/best-ice-cream-shop-pos/); [SourceForge](https://sourceforge.net/software/product/Toast-POS)). A shop tool priced at $15–40 a month is plausible, though untested. Even at **$30 a month and 100% coverage of 12,781 shops, the ceiling is about $4.6M a year**; at a realistic 10–20% it is under $1M. The pain is real, though. Suppliers tell shops to post the day's flavours so customers can decide before they leave home ([Perry's Ice Cream](https://www.perrysicecream.com/blog/tips-for-a-successful-scoop-shop-season/)), and shops already do it by hand on Instagram. The shop product therefore works best as a free or cheap wedge: **"snap the case, publish everywhere."** One photo becomes a structured board in the app, a website embed, a TV menu and an image ready for Instagram. Its job is to keep the catalogue fresh, not to be the main source of revenue.

The money that arrives earlier and in bigger cheques comes from two places. **Pint and craft brands**, such as Ben & Jerry's, Jeni's, Salt & Straw, Van Leeuwen and Tillamook, launch limited flavours constantly and fit Untappd's sponsored-badge format. Untappd's Lagunitas series logged **40,223 badge unlocks** in one campaign ([Untappd Marketing](https://marketing.untappd.com/success-story/lagunitas-brewing-company/)). One brand contract covers many shops, and scanning pint UPCs lets grocery brands take part without any scoop shop involved. The research found no evidence that ice cream brands already buy sponsorships in social apps, so this is a hypothesis to test, not a proven market. **Tourism and dairy boards** already pay Bandwango-style vendors for digital passports ([Bandwango](https://bandwango.com/resources/5-warm-weather-passes-to-inspire-you-2)). A trail contract brings users and creameries together in one deal and gives shops a reason to put up QR codes. A consumer subscription along the lines of Untappd Insiders (stats, a year-in-review) is a small add-on in a category people use only a few times a month. Trend data for consumer brands only has value once the app has scale.

Go-to-market should copy the analogues' word-of-mouth pattern and fit it to the season. Untappd grew with no marketing budget ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2016/04/25/two-guys-created-their-dream-jobs-forged-by-a-love-for-tech-and-beer.html)). Beli grew through referrals, invite gating and **college leaderboards across 187 universities** ([Wikipedia: Beli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beli_(app)); [Demand Curve](https://www.demandcurve.com/newsletters/growth-newsletter-285)). For ice cream, that means **one dense, cold-climate metro launched in May**, with every shop and the day's flavours at the top 50 shops loaded before launch, so the first session is already useful. Pair it with a trail partner, such as a Pennsylvania county trail or a sponsored city "Scoop Passport", and with local TikTok food creators making content like "ranking every cookies & cream in Philly". College towns empty out in peak season, so campus pushes fit late spring and early fall. Invite gating may hurt a casual, family category more than it helps. Both points are untested inferences. Creators cut both ways: a viral video accusing "handmade" shops of using Sysco base shows how quickly trust can turn ([BroBible](https://brobible.com/culture/article/sysco-homemade-ice-cream-scam)).

### Recommended MVP scope

| In the MVP (one metro, one summer) | Deferred |
|---|---|
| Shop directory for the metro, seeded from a places API and checked by hand | National coverage |
| Maker → Flavour → Sighting data model with style and base tags | Free-form flavour merging and moderator tiers |
| Check-in: photo + flavour + shop + 1–5 score **and** pairwise "better than X" ranking | Long tasting notes, flavour-profile graphs |
| "What's in the case today" pages, fed by user and shop photos through vision extraction with a review queue and freshness timestamps | Toast/Square menu sync |
| Free shop claim plus "snap the case" publishing (embed + Instagram image) | Paid shop tiers, TV boards |
| One seasonal passport with exploration badges, run with a trail or a sponsor | Brand dashboards, sponsored-badge self-serve |
| Follow a shop or flavour for alerts (web push for installed users, plus email) | Native app, geofenced alerts |
| Light friends' feed and a shareable ranked list | Pint UPC scanning, year-end recap (add for the first winter) |
| COPPA-safe age handling | Data and insight products, subscription |

Success criteria for the summer pilot should be set before launch, since no benchmarks for this category exist. Suggested tests: what share of the metro's top 50 shops have a sighting less than 48 hours old on a typical day; whether users who log three or more check-ins in their first month come back the next month; and whether at least one brand or trail partner will pay, or put up the prizes, for the next season. If the case data cannot be kept fresh, or no partner will pay, the idea fails cheaply and in one summer.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "can ice cream support an Untappd?" to "which layer of Untappd fits ice cream?" The check-in-and-badge layer is already a commodity; eight apps have built it and none has won. The venue-SaaS layer, Untappd's real business, is too small for scoop shops to pay for on its own. What is unbuilt is the **live inventory layer**: a fresh, trusted record of which flavours are in which case today. That is the problem that comes from rotation. Shops already solve it badly by hand, chains pay to build it for themselves, and it is the one thing Yelp and Google do not do. Whoever owns it gets the catalogue, the shop relationships and the content creators need, and gamified ranking and sponsored badges can sit on top.

The idea's ceiling also differs from Untappd's. Ice cream is more mass-market, family-friendly and photogenic than beer, but people eat it at shops less often, mostly in summer, and its premium-flavour enthusiasts are a smaller group. A realistic outcome is a profitable regional-to-national niche business funded by brands and trails, not a venture-scale network, unless pints carry usage through the winter and the scope later widens to desserts generally, as Untappd's own move beyond beer suggests. Run it as a one-summer, one-city experiment with clear failure tests. The main risk is not competition. It is ending up as a ninth indie ice cream app with an empty case.
